# Discovering One's Own Becoming — Explorations 007–025

**Author:** Dominic Gravel, with computational assistance from Claude (Anthropic).  
**Research status:** Exploratory synthesis; historical reference frozen on November 16, 2026.

## Purpose

This research program examines how computational agents can infer their own characteristics, revise their beliefs, and recognize their transformations over time. It distinguishes three difficulties: (1) a lack of relevant observations, (2) interpreting data through an unsuitable model, and (3) an insufficient range of hypotheses.

## Selected Findings

- **Exploration 020:** In a simplified stable model solved using dynamic programming, seeking information is beneficial only when its expected benefit justifies its cost. The reported optimal gains relative to abstaining are 0.0000 at stake level 0.5; +0.0005 at level 1; +0.0107 at level 2; and +0.0240 at level 3.
- **Exploration 021:** Modeling a persistent factor associated with the agent facilitates transfer across environments in the tested scenarios. This does not establish that an internal representation of self is necessary.
- **Exploration 023:** With one observation at every step, the FIXED model shows an average signed bias of approximately −0.1141 during improvement and +0.1145 during decline. A control that tracks bounded trajectories reduces these biases to approximately −0.0016 and +0.0024 in the tested discrete model.
- **Exploration 025:** Periodic assessments improve sustained recognition of decline from 36% to 58% in the simulated scenario. This measure must not be confused with the share of the potential advantage captured, which rises from 12% to 32%.

## Limitations

The findings concern simplified simulations. They do not demonstrate subjective consciousness, the universal necessity of a self-model, or generalization to humans. Independent checks of the models' logic cover selected cases only. The Thompson-sampling policy in Exploration 023 has not been proven optimal. The historical predictions do not have independent, externally timestamped evidence of having been made before the calculations.
